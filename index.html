Start by identifying what FDA regulates and which center and classification apply.

1 · Define the product

Record intended use, constituent parts, how they are combined, and the product’s primary therapeutic action.

What classification and premarket requirement apply?

FDA overview · not diagram-derived
Scope note. Confirm product classification and lead center first. FDA assignment depends on the product’s primary mode of action and may involve another center. This screening rubric does not determine jurisdiction.
Interactive De Novo rubric

Work through the De Novo decision points

Select regular medical device or combination product first. The first three screens are shared; combination products receive drug-safety and combination-testing screens, while regular devices receive a hardware/software performance screen.

Screening aid only. These questions organize sponsor thinking; they do not determine lead center, pathway eligibility, or FDA’s evidence decision. FDA grants De Novo when general controls, or general and special controls, can provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness for a new device type with no legally marketed predicate. Clinical data needs are case-specific.

01

Is the primary therapeutic action physical or mechanical?

This is the diagram’s first screen for a device-led combination product. A “yes” branch proceeds through the CDRH framework. A “no” branch is marked CDER/CBER and outside this pathway map; confirm lead-center assignment with FDA.

Yes → continue in device-led / CDRH mapNo → CDER / CBER route; outside this map
02

Does the device risk profile require PMA?

The source diagram routes high-risk devices to PMA. This is an initial screen only: verify the device’s classification regulation and FDA requirements before selecting a submission type.

Yes → assess PMA requirementsNo → assess predicate / De Novo route
03

Is there a legally marketed predicate?

Compare intended use and technological characteristics. If a predicate appears available, assess whether the combination product’s drug constituent raises new questions of safety or effectiveness and whether the comparison can support 510(k). An NSE decision may lead to a De Novo assessment when eligibility criteria are met.

Predicate route → assess 510(k)No predicate / NSE → assess De Novo criteria
04

Can general and special controls manage the novel device’s risks?

The source asks whether risks can be controlled through special controls or whether PMA is needed. FDA grants De Novo only when the statutory classification criteria are met; the diagram’s decision question is a planning aid, not an FDA determination.

Controls may be sufficient → continue De Novo assessmentControls not sufficient → assess PMA
05

What evidence gap remains, and are human trials needed?

Plan nonclinical bench and animal work first. The source highlights drug safety, elution kinetics, matrix stability, local toxicity, biological evaluation, combination-specific function, and device performance. It also branches to historical data and clinical testing where evidence gaps remain. The right package is product-specific.

Bench / animal evidence may address the questionRemaining question → evaluate clinical evidence
06

Does the submission support the proposed De Novo classification and controls?

Submit the evidence and proposed controls for FDA review. FDA may grant the request, request additional information, or decline it. Check current eSTAR and submission requirements before filing.

Grant → new Class I or II classificationMore information / decline → respond or reassess pathway
De Novo evidence planning questions

Combination-specific performance

The source lists combined-product function (including injection force, dose accuracy, and release profile), plus stability of the drug in the device configuration and device function over stability time points. Confirm applicable quality requirements.

Bench, biological, and animal evidence

The source identifies drug safety, elution kinetics, matrix stability, local toxicity, biological evaluation, and animal performance. Select studies to address the product-specific risks and intended use; the diagram’s list is not a universal test package.

Clinical evidence when needed

If nonclinical or historical evidence leaves key questions unresolved, plan clinical evidence. The source mentions controlled, self-controlled, and historical-data designs; suitability depends on the question, study design, and FDA feedback.

Supplement types named in the source

30-day notice / 135-day conversion

FDA describes circumstances where a notice may be inadequate and may become a 135-day supplement.

180-day supplement

Used for certain changes affecting safety or effectiveness, including significant changes to components, materials, design, software, or labeling.

Panel-track supplement

For certain significant design or performance changes or a new indication where substantial clinical data are necessary.
